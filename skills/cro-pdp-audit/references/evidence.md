# Evidence library

Research findings and published tests for backing up audit findings.

## How to cite

- **Name the source** and say **what was measured** (clicks ≠ sales; a restaurant study ≠ proof for a product page).
- **Never turn a published result into a forecast** for the user's page. Use it to explain why a fix is worth testing.
- **Respect the tier:**
  - **A** — checked against the original public source.
  - **B** — named, credible source; widely cited. Check the original before using it in public material.
  - **C** — widely repeated but the original study is hard to trace. Say "commonly cited" or leave it out.
- **Benchmarks drift.** Averages (add-to-cart rate, conversion rate) change yearly — say which year or check the current figure.

## Images

| Finding | Tier | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Product images rank among the most influential purchase factors in US shopper surveys (83% in one widely cited survey). | B | eMarketer survey data |
| A large share of shoppers (about 42% in testing) try to judge a product's size from its images — so in-scale images matter. | B | Baymard Institute — product page UX research |
| Many sites still don't show customer or social photos on the product page (about two-thirds in Baymard's benchmark). | B | Baymard Institute — product page benchmark |
| Close-up images get more engagement than zoomed-out ones (≈ +24%). | C | Taboola trends data, commonly cited |

**Must-have image types** (from e-commerce image guidance): standalone, close-up, in context (on a model or in use), side/alternate view, color/variant images — plus in-scale and customer photos. On mobile: swipeable carousel, position indicator, pinch-to-zoom.

## Reviews and social proof

| Finding | Tier | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Shoppers who see a product page with 1+ reviews convert 52.2% more than those who see none; 11–30 reviews more than doubles conversion; 101+ reviews lifts it over 250%. | B | [PowerReviews — Impact of review volume on conversion](https://www.powerreviews.com/review-volume-conversion-impact/) |
| The effect of reviews grows with price: about +19% conversion for lower-priced products vs about +380% for higher-priced ones. | B | Spiegel Research Center (Northwestern), *How Online Reviews Influence Sales* |
| Products with ~5 reviews are nearly 4× (≈ +270%) more likely to be purchased than products with none. | B | Spiegel Research Center (Northwestern) |
| Purchase likelihood peaks at an average rating of 4.2–4.5 stars and falls as ratings approach 5.0 — perfect scores look too good to be true. | B | [Northwestern / Spiegel Research Center](https://news.northwestern.edu/stories/2016/01/opinion-techcrunch-reviews) |
| Most shoppers actively look for negative reviews before buying (≈ 82%). | B | Spiegel Research Center (Northwestern) |
| 91% of 18–34-year-olds trust online reviews as much as personal recommendations. | B | [BrightLocal — Local Consumer Review Survey](https://www.brightlocal.com/learn/local-consumer-review-survey) |
| 92% of consumers trust recommendations from people they know over advertising; online reviews are among the most trusted ad formats. | B | Nielsen Global Trust in Advertising (2012, 2015) |
| A one-star increase in a restaurant's Yelp rating led to a 5–9% increase in revenue. | B | Michael Luca, Harvard Business School (2011) — **restaurants, not e-commerce** |

**18 kinds of social proof** (KlientBoost): testimonials, reviews, star ratings, case studies, client logos, awards, press mentions, wisdom of the crowd, wisdom of friends, customer data, integrations, years in business, research, trust badges, endorsements, user-generated content, recent-sales notifications, frequently bought together.

**When to ask for reviews:** at emotional high points — a reorder, a brand tag on social, a positive DM or comment, right after support solves a problem.

## CTAs and buttons

| Finding | Tier | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Veeam changed a product-page link from "Request a quote" to "Request pricing". Click-through to the sales inquiry page went from 0.54% to 1.40% (+161.66%). | **A** | [VWO case study (PDF)](https://static.wingify.com/vwo/uploads/2019/06/veeam-software-increased-conversions_2019-06-29-19-30-54.pdf) |
| Changing "your" to "my" in a CTA increased conversions 90%. | B | Unbounce test, cited in [KlientBoost — Landing page copy](https://www.klientboost.com/landing-pages/landing-page-copy/) |
| Rounded buttons drew higher click-through than sharp-cornered ones across several field studies (+17% to +55% depending on context). | B | Biswas, Abell & Chacko — peer-reviewed research on rounded vs angular buttons |
| About 90% of visitors who read a headline also read the CTA. | B | Unbounce, cited by Constant Content |
| A one-click 10%-off exit offer shown to shoppers leaving the cart produced about $163.6K in sales over 30 days for one online store (BlendJet). | **A** | [BDOW! (formerly Sumo) case study](https://bdow.com/stories/shopify-sales/) |

**Standards:** Apple Human Interface Guidelines — minimum tap target 44×44 pt. WCAG 2.1 AA — text contrast 4.5:1 (3:1 for large text).

## Not included on purpose

- "Multiple offers plummet conversion by 266%" — a decrease can't exceed 100%; the claim's wording or method doesn't hold up.
- Vague "studies show" figures with no traceable source.

If you add a finding, add its source and tier.
