# Scoring anchors for the 12 product page sections

Score each section against its anchors, not by impression. Supporting data for every "basis" line is in `evidence.md`.

---

## 1. Hero / above the fold (12)

**Tests:** within about 8 seconds, can a visitor answer: what is it? who is it for? what do I get? how much? do other people like it?

**Five must-haves** (each missing one costs 1.5–2 points):
1. **Product title** that names the category and promise (not just a model number).
2. **Subheadline** that adds context, audience or benefit.
3. **First image** — the most compelling angle; for visual products usually in context rather than a plain cut-out.
4. **Price** — including compare-at / sale price when relevant.
5. **Rating anchor** — "4.6★ from [N] reviews".

**Bonus items** (+0.5 each, capped at 12): variant swatches above the fold · CTA above the fold · shipping cost/timing ("Free shipping over $X, ships in 24h") · genuine scarcity label · a risk-reversal line ("60-day returns").

**Anchors:**
- **12** — All five + 3 or more bonus items + a subheadline that names the specific audience or use.
- **10** — All five + 1–2 bonus items.
- **8** — All five, weak subheadline.
- **6** — One must-have missing (most often the rating).
- **4** — Two missing (typically rating + subheadline).
- **2** — Only image + title.
- **0** — No discernible hero.

**Common deductions:** title is a brand or model name (−2) · subheadline restates the title (−1.5) · price off-screen or tiny (−1) · reviews exist but no rating in the hero (−2) · hero chases 3+ emotions at once (−2, worse on mobile) · image so bright it drowns the text (−1).

**Example (10/12, fictional):**
> Title: "Keeps coffee hot through your longest meeting"
> Subheadline: "20 oz double-wall travel mug — leakproof lid, fits car cup holders"
> Image: in hand on a commute, steam visible
> Price: ~~$38~~ **$32**
> Rating: 4.6★ from [real review count] reviews
> Bonus: sticky "Add to cart — free shipping over $50"

## 2. Image gallery (12)

**Tests:** quantity, variety of angles, in-scale, customer photos, mobile usability.

**Basis:** product images rank among the top purchase factors in shopper surveys; a large share of shoppers try to judge size from images (Baymard); close-ups drive more engagement than zoomed-out shots.

**Five must-have image types** (−1.5 each if missing):
1. **Standalone** — clean background, whole product.
2. **Close-up** — material, finish, craftsmanship.
3. **In-scale** — in a hand, on a person, next to a familiar object.
4. **In-context** — in use, in its real environment.
5. **Customer photos (UGC)** — 3–9 real photos.

**Bonus:** every variant/color shown (swatches beat text) · 360° view · short GIF of a moving part · video thumbnail inside the gallery.

**Anchors:**
- **12** — 8+ images, all five types, customer photos, swipeable on mobile with a position indicator.
- **10** — 5+ images, one type missing, swipeable.
- **8** — 5+ images, two types missing (usually UGC + in-scale).
- **6** — 3–4 images, one studio style, few angles.
- **4** — 2 images; product fills less than ~60% of the frame.
- **2** — 1 image, and it's a generic supplier photo.
- **0** — No images.

**Common deductions:** tiny variations of one angle (−2) · obvious generic supplier/marketplace photos (−3, a trust issue) · no in-scale image (−2, especially for higher-priced items) · models clearly don't match the buyer (−1.5) · over-retouched (−1) · mobile carousel with no indicator of how many images (−1).

**Order tip:** spec-driven products lead with a crisp product shot; visual-driven products lead with an in-context lifestyle shot. Wrong order: −1.

## 3. Product video (8)

**Tests:** presence, type, placement, first 3 seconds, captions.

**Video types that work on a PDP:** explainer · **how-to / demo** (usually the most effective) · unboxing · comparison with alternatives. Brand-story films belong on About pages, not PDPs.

**21 product-video rules** (cite the ones the page breaks): script it · place it high · cut distractions · show the product in use · optimize for search · keep it short · custom thumbnail · custom player color · avoid autoplay (but test) · sell less, teach more · friendly and personal · **closed captions** (most mobile video is watched muted) · **make the first 3 seconds count** · style that fits the market · produce cheaply to reduce risk · back claims with social proof · video as hero background (rarely) · show how the buyer gets value · touch on the unique value proposition · video and CTA should match · **limit to 1–2 videos per page**. *(Adapted from KlientBoost's landing-page video guidance.)*

**Anchors:**
- **8** — Demo video above the fold or 2nd in the gallery + strong first 3 seconds + captions + custom thumbnail + 30–60 seconds.
- **6** — Video exists but sits mid-page or lower.
- **4** — Over 60 seconds, not mobile-friendly, or opens flat ("Welcome to Brand X").
- **2** — Only an embedded third-party video, nothing made for this page.
- **0** — No video.

**Common deductions:** a brand ad instead of a product demo (−2 to −3) · autoplay with sound (−2) · black or awkward auto-thumbnail (−1) · no captions (−1.5).

## 4. Benefit > feature copy (15 — highest weight)

**Tests:** whether the description and bullets say what the buyer **gets**, not only what the product **has**.

**Basis:** the classic "This vacuum has 2000W power" → "Spend less time cleaning and more time with your family"; KlientBoost's advice to keep landing-page copy "short on features, long on benefits".

**Vitamin vs painkiller:**
- **Vitamin** (benefits over time — décor, accessories, hobbies, home goods): sell the compounding good, "built to last".
- **Painkiller** (fixes something now — cleaning, repair, relief): sell the pain disappearing.
- Wrong strategy for the category: **−3 to −4**.

| Feature | Benefit |
|---|---|
| Double-wall vacuum insulation | Coffee still hot at your 11am meeting |
| Leakproof lid | Toss it in your bag next to your laptop |
| 304 stainless steel | Still rust-free in ten years |
| 2000W motor | The whole house in 12 minutes |

*(Numbers in example rewrites are illustrative — use only figures the seller can back up.)*

**Anchors:**
- **15** — Every feature translated; second person; no jargon; right vitamin/painkiller strategy.
- **12** — ~80% translated.
- **9** — Half features, half benefits.
- **6** — Feature list with the odd empty benefit ("high quality").
- **3** — Pure spec sheet.
- **0** — Strategy backwards (a vitamin sold as a painkiller or vice versa).

**Common deductions:** each empty claim like "premium materials", "innovative design" (−1) · untranslated specs (−2 to −3) · jargon (−2) · no "you" anywhere (−1).

**Spec-sheet exception:** spec-driven products need a spec table; judge its scannability under section 9.

## 5. Social proof (12)

**Tests:** rating anchor, review count, photo reviews, review detail, recency, believability.

**Basis:** PowerReviews (reviews lift conversion, more so at higher prices and higher volume); Spiegel Research Center (≈5 reviews → purchase likelihood ~4×; ratings peak around 4.2–4.5 stars); BrightLocal and others on trust in reviews.

**Five kinds of proof** (at least 3 to pass):
1. **Statistic anchor** — "4.6★ from [N] reviews" (belongs in the hero).
2. **Representative reviews** — photo, name, location, date, "Verified buyer".
3. **Customer photo gallery** — 3–9 real photos.
4. **Press mentions** — bonus, not required.
5. **Third-party review platform** — link or widget from an independent platform.

**Anchors:**
- **12** — 3+ kinds incl. customer photos, detailed reviews and a third-party platform; average rating in the believable range (~4.2–4.7); reviews within the last 3 months.
- **10** — 3 kinds incl. customer photos.
- **8** — 2 kinds (usually rating + text), no photos.
- **6** — Only a rating; reviews look polished.
- **4** — ≤ 5 reviews, or every review is 5 stars.
- **2** — Only empty template reviews ("⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Great product").
- **0** — No social proof anywhere.

**Common deductions:** "5.0★ from 4 reviews" (−2) · no reviews below 5 stars (−2) · no "Verified buyer" labels (−1) · no names/photos/locations (−1) · nothing newer than ~2 years (−1.5) · no customer photos (−2, worse for visual products) · no sorting by recent or helpful (−1).

**Tip:** if the store has fewer than ~30 reviews, add a review-collection push to next steps — ask at emotional high points (a reorder, a tag on social, after support solves a problem).

## 6. FAQ (5)

**Tests:** presence, structure, coverage of high-frequency questions, a way to get further help.

**Five must-answer questions:**
1. Shipping cost and delivery time.
2. Returns — can I send it back if I don't like it?
3. Compatibility / fit — "Does this fit my …?"
4. Care / maintenance — "How do I clean or store it?"
5. The category-specific question buyers always ask (capacity, sizing, materials, safety…).

**Anchors:**
- **5** — All five + clear grouping + a contact option with response time.
- **4** — Four of five + grouping.
- **3** — Three, no grouping.
- **2** — One or two questions.
- **1** — FAQ exists but is empty fluff ("Is this good? Yes!").
- **0** — No FAQ.

**Common deductions:** FAQ only in the footer (−1) · hidden in horizontal tabs (−1) · 200+ word answers (−0.5) · no "Still have questions?" exit (−0.5).

## 7. Trust & risk reversal (8)

**Tests:** visible returns, shipping transparency, payment and security cues, contact info, guarantee language, honest scarcity.

**Basis:** Baymard (returns and trust as abandonment reasons; Norton seal rated most trusted in its badge study); KoMarketing (visitors want contact info and leave without it); Stanford Web Credibility Research.

**Checklist:**
- 📦 Returns policy visible on the page (30 days minimum; 60–365 is a strong signal).
- 🚚 Shipping cost and delivery estimate before checkout.
- 💳 Payment method icons, including wallets and pay-later where offered.
- 🔒 Data reassurance where you collect info ("we'll never share your email").
- 📞 Contact: email + hours at minimum; chat near the CTA is a bonus.
- 🛡️ Guarantee language ("60-day money-back guarantee").
- 🏆 Third-party badges — real certifications, real press.
- 📍 Genuine scarcity (handmade batch, seasonal, numbered run) if it exists.

**Anchors:**
- **8** — All eight, with the guarantee right by the CTA.
- **6** — One or two missing.
- **4** — Three or four missing.
- **2** — Only payment icons.
- **0** — No trust elements, or fake scarcity.

**Common deductions:** returns only in the footer (−2) · fake countdown or fake "only X left" (−3 and 🔴) · "Loved by 100,000+ customers" with no source (−1) · contact without hours or response time (−1) · self-made "quality guaranteed" badges instead of third-party ones (−1).

## 8. CTA & add to cart (10)

**Tests:** copy, placement, sticky behavior, contrast, number of competing CTAs, feedback on tap.

**Five checks:**
1. **Copy** — verb-led with a clear benefit ("Add to cart — ships today").
2. **Placement** — above the fold + sticky bottom bar on mobile.
3. **Size** — at least 44×44 px on mobile (Apple's guideline), ideally full width.
4. **Contrast** — stands out from the background (not just "on brand").
5. **Closer line** — a small risk-reducing line beneath ("Free returns for 60 days · Free shipping over $X").

**Anchors:**
- **10** — All five + rounded corners + tap feedback.
- **8** — Four of five.
- **6** — Three of five (typically right place, weak copy).
- **4** — A bare "Buy now" / "Submit".
- **2** — Small, low-contrast or off-screen.
- **0** — No CTA, or an image that isn't a real button.

**Banned CTA copy** (−1 each): "Submit" · "Click here" · "Learn more" · "OK" · bare "Buy now" · ALL CAPS · Title Case (use sentence case: "Add to cart").

**Templates:** "Add to cart — $X · free shipping over $Y" · "Sign me up — $5 off my first order" · "Order now — free 60-day returns" · "Get my [product]".

**Common deductions:** three or more equal-weight CTAs competing (−2) · no sticky CTA on mobile (−2) · weak contrast (−1.5) · buttons closer than ~2 mm on mobile (−1) · no tap feedback (−0.5).

## 9. Design & visual hierarchy (8)

**Tests:** contrast, type size and count, hierarchy, whitespace, reading pattern.

**Five checks:**
1. Text contrast ≥ **4.5:1** (WCAG 2.1 AA; 3:1 for large text).
2. Mobile body text ≥ **16px**.
3. **≤ 2 typefaces**, no decorative fonts for body text.
4. Clear **hierarchy** — headline, subhead and body differ in size, weight and color.
5. Enough **whitespace** — cramped spacing adds cognitive strain.

**Anchors:**
- **8** — All five + ≤ 3 main colors + a clear F- or Z-pattern + no overload on mobile.
- **6** — Four of five.
- **4** — Three of five.
- **2** — One or two; poor contrast; Title Case everywhere.
- **0** — No design: stacked large text, no hierarchy.

**Common deductions:** mobile body < 16px (−2) · weak size difference between levels (−1.5) · 3+ typefaces (−2) · contrast below 4.5:1 (−1.5) · no paragraph spacing (−1) · description/specs/reviews in horizontal tabs (−2).

**Spec tables:** ≤ 20 specs fit one column; more need grouping, subheads or icons; two-column spec layouts are harder to scan (−1).

## 10. Mobile experience (5)

**Seven checks:**
1. Loads in under ~3 seconds (check Core Web Vitals).
2. Body text ≥ 16px.
3. CTA ≥ 44×44 px, ideally full width.
4. ≥ 2 mm between tap targets.
5. Sticky bottom CTA.
6. Pinch-to-zoom on images.
7. Non-intrusive pop-ups (no full-screen takeover with a hidden close button).

**Anchors:** **5** all seven · **4** one missing (usually sticky CTA) · **3** two or three missing · **2** four missing · **0** unusable on mobile.

**Common deductions:** desktop layout squeezed onto mobile (−2) · full-screen pop-up with hard-to-find close (−2) · no zoom (−1) · desktop mega-menu on mobile (−1).

## 11. Marketing hooks (3)

**Options:** genuine scarcity ("only 50 made this batch") · seasonal relevance · decoy pricing (a pricier option that makes the main one look like the smart buy) · auto-applied bundle discounts ("Buy 2, save 15% — applied at checkout") · real sales data ("500+ sold this week", if true).

**Anchors:**
- **3** — One or two genuine hooks, not stacked.
- **2** — Hooks that don't fit the category (painkiller urgency on a vitamin product).
- **1** — No hooks (a missed opportunity).
- **0** — Fake countdowns or fake stock counts.

**Tip:** vitamin products shouldn't force urgency — use "a classic", "built to last". Painkillers can use time pressure.

---

## 12. Upsell / cross-sell (2)

- **2** — "Frequently bought together", "complete the set", or a bundle with the saving stated.
- **1** — Only a generic "You may also like".
- **0** — No cross-sell.

**Deductions:** irrelevant recommendations (−0.5) · bundle with no stated saving (−0.5).

---

## Totals

Add the 12 scores. In the verdict, name the **2–3 weakest sections and the single highest-ROI step**. Two pages scoring 60 can need completely different fixes, so never summarize with the total alone.

- **90–100** — Textbook; fine-tune with A/B tests.
- **75–89** — Strong; 1–2 sections to improve.
- **60–74** — Passable; 3–4 sections with clear problems.
- **45–59** — 5+ weak sections; consider a structured redesign.
- **30–44** — Hurting conversion; start with social proof, benefits and CTA.
- **0–29** — Missing the basics.
